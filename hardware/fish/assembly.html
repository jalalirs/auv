Fish v1 Assembly

Ten steps from the parts ordered on 19 September to a fish swimming in the tank with the DGX Spark driving it. Each step says what it uses, what you do, and what has to be true before the next one starts. Nothing is skipped: the bench tests come first because a motor that bogs or a Pi that will not stream is cheaper to find on a desk than inside a sealed head.

1desk

Bench the drivetrain parts ordered

Wire the 12 V supply to the BTS7960, the driver to the motor, and the ESP32 you already own to the driver's two PWM pins. Run the motor forward and reverse from a ten-line sketch. Then clamp a 30 mm lever on the shaft, press it on the scale, and read the stall torque.

Uses motor · BTS7960 · 12 V supply · ESP32 · breadboard kit · scale
Done when the motor reverses cleanly and holds at least 0.8 kg·cm at 12 V. Below that, order the 37 mm 300 rpm motor before printing anything.
2desk

Bring the Pi up on the desk parts ordered

Flash Pi OS Lite, plug the camera in with the 22-pin ribbon, the BNO055 on I²C, the Ethernet adapter into the micro-USB port, and power it from the buck set to 5.1 V. Stream the camera to your laptop over the 15 m Cat6 and print the IMU's heading beside it. This is also where the ROS 2 nodes for the fish get written, before there is a fish.

Uses Pi Zero 2 W · camera · 22-pin cable · Ethernet adapter · BNO055 · buck · Cat6 · 12 V supply
Done when the laptop shows live video and heading over the full tether length, and the Pi drives the BTS7960 from a ROS 2 topic.
3cad

Redraw the head, print at Sketchat my work

OpenFish's head is built around a Pololu motor and a right-angle gearbox we cannot buy here. It gets redrawn in build123d around the 25GA-370: the crank sits directly on its shaft, the camera looks out of a flat acrylic window in the nose, the tether enters through a PG7 boss beside it, and the two halves meet on a gasket groove. The tail ribs and the tail mould come from OpenFish unchanged. Everything goes to Sketchat in PETG.

Prints head top · head bottom · crank · 6 tail ribs · 2 mould halves · gasket tray
Done when the motor, Pi, driver, buck and IMU all fit inside the printed head with the lid on, dry, and the halves close flat on the groove.
4hands

Cast the tail silicone ordered

Thread the six ribs onto the two steel cables at OpenFish's spacings, lay them in the mould, close it, and pour the silicone mixed 1:1 through the top. Tap the mould to shed bubbles. Four hours later, open it. Pour the leftover into the flat tray: that sheet is the head gasket.

Uses BBDINO silicone · steel wire · printed ribs and mould
Done when the tail bends smoothly to 45° either side with a finger on the cable and springs back, and there are no voids at the rib edges.
5hands

Fit the drivetrain after printing

Motor into its mount, crank pressed onto the D-shaft, the tail's two cables through the guides and clamped to the crank at opposite points. Turn the crank by hand: the tail should swing fully left and fully right once per revolution. Adjust cable length until both sides reach the same angle. Then run step 1's sketch and watch it beat at 8 Hz in air.

Uses motor · printed crank and guides · the cast tail
Done when the tail beats symmetrically at full speed in air for five minutes without a cable slipping or the motor warming past hand-hot.
6hands

Install the electronics after printing

Camera against the nose window, Pi and buck amidships, driver and IMU beside the motor, IMU flat with its x axis forward. The tether comes in through the PG7 gland: the Cat6 and the two power cores both pass through it, with a bead of silicone around the bundle. Two bare wires on the floor of the head go to a GPIO pin; water across them is the leak alarm.

Uses everything from step 2 · PG7 gland · the head
Done when with the lid off, video, IMU and tail all work over the tether, and the leak pin reads dry.
7bucket

Seal and leak-test the head after printing

Gasket on the groove, lid on, eight M3 screws tightened in a star pattern. A folded paper tissue sits inside against the floor. Sink the head in a bucket with the tether's surface end unplugged and leave it an hour. Open it. Do this before the first swim, and again after any time the head is opened.

Uses gasket sheet from step 4 · M3 screws · a bucket
Done when the tissue is dry after an hour at the bottom of the bucket. A damp corner means the gasket or the gland, and the fish does not swim until it is dry.
8bucket

Trim it weights ordered

Whole fish in the bucket, powered off. It will float high, head up, because the sealed head is the only air in it. Stick 5 g strips under the head until the fish hangs level just under the surface and rises at about a centimetre a second when you let go. Weigh it on the scale before and after; the difference is what the simulator's model will need.

Uses adhesive weights · scale · a bucket
Done when it floats level, barely positive, and stays that way for ten minutes. A fish that sinks when the power fails is a fish you have to go and get.
9tank

First swim, on the tether controller is code

Tank filled, tether to the DGX, a phone or webcam clamped above the tank looking straight down. The first controller is a sine wave: tail amplitude and frequency as ROS 2 parameters, start at 1 Hz, go up in steps. The overhead camera records the track. From it you get speed per frequency, the turn radius when the wave is offset to one side, and whether the fish rolls.

Uses the fish · tether · DGX Spark · overhead camera · the tank
Done when the fish crosses the tank on its own, the track is recorded, and a plot of speed against tail frequency exists.
10record

Measure it for the simulator my work

Mass on the scale. Volume by lowering it into a full jug and weighing what spills. Thrust by tethering the nose to the scale through a pulley and running the tail. Drag from how fast it sinks with a known weight added. Centre of gravity from where it balances on a pencil. These six numbers become the fish's entry in the vehicle catalogue, and the other session gets a vehicle whose coefficients somebody actually measured.

Uses scale · a jug · a pulley · the step 9 plots
Done when the simulator's fish, given the same tail command, crosses the virtual tank in the same time as the real one did, within a fifth.
