# A numerical tow tank: mini-hoot's and Luna's drag, 5 October 2026

`hardware/tow_tank.py voxels <hull.usd> <out.npz> --axis surge` · `hardware/tow_tank.py run <out.npz | cube:32 | sphere:48>` · results in `hardware/towed.json`

Nobody has towed mini-hoot or Boxfish Luna. Their drag came from Hoerner's closed-body coefficients applied to their silhouettes, and the panel model (docs/results/hull-panels-2026-10-04.md) disagreed with that by a factor of two. So the water round each hull was solved instead.

The solver is a lattice Boltzmann method written in Warp and run on the box's GPU:

- D3Q19 lattice, with a Smagorinsky large-eddy model for the turbulence the grid cannot resolve.
- Half-way bounce-back walls, and the force taken by momentum exchange.
- Water entering at a set speed and leaving downstream; the tank is periodic across.
- Hulls 70 to 113 cells long, filling 4 to 5 % of the cross-section. Each run is three flow-throughs, with the last one averaged.

## Is it worth anything?

Only as far as it reproduces what was measured:

| body | solver | measured | |
|---|---|---|---|
| cube, face on | Cd 1.27 | 1.05 (Hoerner) | +21 % |
| sphere | Cd 0.62 | 0.47 (Hoerner, Achenbach) | +32 % |
| BlueROV2 Heavy, surge 1 m/s | 51.3 N | ≈ 45 N (Li et al. 2020, load cells) | +14 % |
| BlueROV2 Heavy, sway 0.6 m/s | 26.1 N | ≈ 30 N (Li et al. 2020) | −13 % |

It reads high on idealised shapes. On the one real vehicle measured, it is within about 14 % and biased neither way. The hull results below are therefore taken as they come, good to about 15 %. The BlueROV2 numbers are the Heavy's mesh against the standard frame Li measured, which differ a little.

## How the solver got there

These runs are recorded so they are not repeated:

- **Plain BGK collision** matched best: a cube +13 % and a sphere +6 %, at Re 5×10⁴. But it blew up on every real hull. The Heavy's frame plates are two to three cells thick, and mini-hoot's thruster ducts have lips that fine. This happened at Re 5×10⁴, at 2×10⁴ and at 10⁴.
- **The regularised collision** (Latt & Chopard 2006) is stable on all of them, at the cost of the bias above. Mini-hoot's sway still went unstable late at Re 5×10⁴ and was run at 2×10⁴, where bluff-body drag is nearly the same.
- **Blockage.** The same cube at 4 % and 1.5 % of the section agreed within the run-to-run scatter, so the periodic tank is not crowding the flow enough to correct for.
- **Two faults in the tool itself, fixed.** Every cell beside the hull adding its force to one total each step queued them all on one address, and made the measured flow-through thirty times slower than the rest. The total is now summed over 4,096 slots. A slot hash that overflowed 32 bits was also caught before it ran.

## What the hulls came out at

Quadratic drag, N/(m/s)², against what the packages carried (Hoerner on the silhouette) and the panel model:

| | tow tank | silhouette (was) | panels (open-frame wake) |
|---|---|---|---|
| mini-hoot surge | **9.9** | 12.4 | 26 |
| mini-hoot sway | **18.4** | 18.8 | 46 |
| mini-hoot heave | **29.7** | 31.0 | 48 |
| Luna surge | **59.5** | 54.6 | 80 |
| Luna sway | **104.6** | 105.6 | 46 |
| Luna heave | **112.9** | 123.5 | 47 |

The silhouette estimate held: within 20 % on every axis, and within 5 % on four of the six. The panel model with the open frame's wake was wrong for closed hulls, as suspected. Mini-hoot's rounded hull is a little slipperier ahead than a bluff body of its shape (Cd 0.68 against Hoerner's 0.88).

Both packages now carry the towed numbers (`hardware/hull_coefficients.towed_for`; their package scripts prefer it). Rotations stay the BlueROV2's scaled.

## Still not measured

A real tow of mini-hoot would replace all of this, and is what the 15 % says is owed. The tool can also put the Heavy's frame on a finer grid once there is GPU memory for it. At 160 cells the plates would be four cells thick, which BGK may hold.
