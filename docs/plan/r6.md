# The sixth plan: an ocean where things act on each other

r5 made the platform something an agent can drive. The tank work at the end of
it — mini-hoot, iocean-tank-1, the path-traced round trip — showed what the
picture can be on the box's GPUs, and it also showed what is missing behind the
picture.

What is there today is parts that each read the vehicle and nothing else.

- The fish see the vehicle, and the vehicle's sonar does not see the fish.
- The thrusters push the vehicle, and nothing else feels them.
- The tether is a shape redrawn each tick, not a cable with weight and drag.
- A coral sways in the current, and a vehicle sitting on it does nothing to it.

That is a set of good-looking pieces. It is not an ocean.

**r6 is one engine where the physical systems act on each other, in real time,
drawn at the quality the round-trip film reached.** A thruster's wash moves the
water. The water moves the fish, the coral, the sand and the cable. The fish get
out of the way, or do not and are struck. The cable fouls on the rock and the
dive fails because of it.

## Why this, and not more features

As far as a first search shows, nobody has built this.

- **HoloOcean 2.0** (Unreal 5.3) has good vehicle dynamics and sonar.
- **Stonefish** (C++, Bullet) has the best physics, including a lumped-mass
  cable.
- **OceanSim** (Isaac Sim, the same base as ours) has fast camera and sonar
  rendering.

None of them has fish with behaviour, living coral, a water field the vehicle
stirs, or a tether that can tangle. Each of those exists as a paper on its own.
Bringing them into one engine for the first time is the new thing.

It is also what sells. The September assessment still stands: a simulator
licence on its own does not sell. A coupled reef answers questions the buyers
actually have, and nothing else can answer them before a dive:

- how much does this vehicle bias the fish count by frightening them off?
- does its wash smother or break the coral it is surveying?
- will its cable foul on this reef?

If item 0 finds that someone has already built this, the plan changes: we build
on theirs or sell the parts to KAUST cheaply, and do not race.

## How the code is laid out

The user has to be able to read it, so this comes before any physics.

**One clock and one world state.** Each system is its own module:

- vehicle
- water
- fish
- coral
- tether
- sediment
- light
- contact
- each sensor

Each module declares **what it reads and what it writes**. The engine steps
them in an order it derives from those declarations, not one written by hand.

A system can be read, and tested, without opening any other. Its tests check it
against published numbers, not against how it looks.

Heavy work goes onto the GPU as **NVIDIA Warp** kernels. Warp kernels are
written in Python and compiled to CUDA, and Warp already ships inside Isaac Sim.
This covers the water field, the cable segments, the sediment particles and
neighbour search across thousands of fish. Python stays as the glue. We drop to
C++ only where Warp falls short, and only after measuring that it does.

**Real time** holds for the simulation and the live view. A path-traced film is
slower than real time and stays an offline render of the same run.

Every coupling says what it is: measured, derived, chosen or assumed, as every
number on this platform already does.

## 0. What has been done before

A proper survey, not the ten minutes behind the paragraph above. Simulators,
fish-behaviour models, cable models, coral mechanics. For each one, write down
what it has, what licence it is under, and whether we could use it rather than
write it.

**Done when:** a written comparison exists, and it says plainly whether the
coupled engine is new.

**Done, 2 October.** See [`r6-prior-art.md`](r6-prior-art.md). About 25
simulators were checked, academic and commercial. None has water the vehicle
stirs, and none has coral that bends or breaks. The closest is Stonefish with
its fish add-on, and it lacks both.

What we adopt:

- **Fish:** Calovi/Lei swimming and schooling; Hein's looming rule for fleeing.
- **Wash:** Hamill/Verhey jets.
- **Coral:** Madin's shape factor for being torn off; *Acropora* strengths for
  breaking.
- **Tether:** a cable on Newton (NVIDIA's physics engine on Warp), checked
  against the Genoa/DFKI tether dataset.
- **Tether failure:** REACT's test, plus a held-vehicle test.

## 1. The engine

The layout above, with what exists today moved into it. Nothing new gets built
yet; the work is getting the existing parts into modules:

- the vehicle's dynamics
- the water and the wind
- the tether
- the fish
- the sway
- the sonar and the other sensors

The runner shrinks to the loop that steps them.

**Done when:** the round trip in iocean-tank-1 flies the same as it does today
(same score, same trajectory to the millimetre on the same seed). Each system
is a module with its reads and writes declared, and the step order is printed
from those declarations.

## 2. The picture, kept and taken further

What the film reached becomes the standard:

- physical materials
- grown textures
- path-traced film mode
- fixed views

On top of that:

- **Thrusters spin at their commanded RPM.** The propeller you see turning is
  the one the controller is driving, at the speed it is driving it, in both
  directions.
- **No dropped frames.** The film is as long as the dive. Today the recorder
  drops frames and the video comes out short.
- **A live view** in real-time RTX, alongside the path-traced film of the same
  run.

**Done when:** a film of the round trip shows each propeller turning visibly
with its command, and the film is the length of the dive.

### The live view and the film of the same run (3 October)

The live view exists: an interactive dive is drawn in real-time RTX and sent to
the watcher as frames over the dive's websocket (`watch.py`). What was missing
is the film *of that run*. A path-traced frame takes seconds, so the film cannot
be made while somebody flies; and a person at the keys, or a stack over ROS 2,
cannot be asked to fly the same dive twice.

What they commanded can be. Every command enters the physics at one place
(`world.asked.commands`, once a tick), so the helm now keeps every tick's
command, the recording writes them as `commands.npy`, and a brief carrying
`"replay": {"commands": <that file>}` plays them back in place of asking
anybody. The same seed and the same commands are the same dive, tick for tick:
`test_replay.py` holds it to exact equality of every position. So a run watched
live gets its path-traced film afterwards — the same trajectory, the same fish,
the same sand — flown undrawn-fast up to the film's frames.

**Built 4 October, on the user's yes.**
The platform half: a dive asked for as `filmOf: <run>` is queued as a drawn,
filmed run whose brief carries `replay`, and the worker is handed that run's
`commands.npy` the way it is handed the place and the vehicle (`RunPackages`).
Today a run sees its own two packages and nothing else; this lets a film run
read one file of the run it films, owned by the same account. Control plane:
the field, the check that both runs are the requester's, and the artifact handed
over; worker: fetch it into the brief directory; MCP: `filmOf` on `dives_start`.

## 3. One water field

Current, tide and the pump jet in a tank, the wind on the surface, wave surge,
and **the vehicle's own thruster wash**: all in one field on the GPU that
everything else reads.

- the vehicle's drag
- the fish, which are carried by it and turn into it
- the coral sway
- the marine snow
- the sediment
- the cable

The thrusters write into it. The field is a reduced-order model, a background
plus jets and wakes; it is not CFD. It says so.

**Done when:**

- a vehicle hovering over sand sends a measurable jet down into it
- a fish behind the vehicle is pushed by the wash
- a sea fan in front of a thruster bends away from it

## 4. Contact

Real collision between the vehicle, the rock, the coral, the glass, the fish and
the cable. Contact gives forces, not just a count. A strike has a place, a
speed and an impulse, and the dive records all three.

**Done when:** the vehicle driven into the pillar stops, rebounds and records
the impulse; and a fish struck by it is knocked aside and recorded.

## 5. The fish system

Started in r5 as `coral/fishmind.py` and `coral/fish_species.py`. It is not
wired into the runtime yet and not committed.

- **A sheet per species** (FishBase): size, swimming, schooling, territory,
  home range, height off the bottom, when it is active, where it sleeps, diet,
  flight distance.
- **A mind per fish** (Tu & Terzopoulos): hunger, fear and the urge to be
  active, plus a personality. Together they choose rest, forage, school,
  patrol, wander or flee, and the fish holds that intention for a while.
- **A day:** daylight on a reef, the light schedule in a tank. Day fish go to
  their refuge at dusk; night fish come out.
- **Burst-and-coast swimming** with data-fitted attraction and alignment to the
  nearest one to three of their own kind (Calovi 2018, Lei 2020).
- **Senses:** the vehicle as a threat by distance, approach and noise; rock and
  coral as things to steer round. Today they swim through both.
- **Seen by the sonar** as echoes, and counted by the camera against the true
  number present.
- **Colours and markings per species.**

**Done when:** tests against published ranges pass:

- kick interval
- cruising speed in body lengths a second
- nearest-neighbour distance and alignment
- night spent in a refuge
- flight distance
- containment

A dive reports what each species spent its time doing, and how many fish the
camera counted against how many were there.

## 6. The coral system

Coral as a living, physical thing on the seabed:

- **It bends** with the water field, by growth form and stiffness.
- **It breaks** when struck past its strength. Branching coral is fragile,
  massive coral is not.
- **It is smothered** where sediment settles on it.

Every one of those is recorded against the colony, so a dive can report the
damage it did.

**Done when:** a vehicle that lands on a branching colony breaks it, and the
dive's result lists the colony, the force, and the fragments.

## 7. The tether, as a cable

A lumped-mass cable, in the line of Stonefish: segments with mass, buoyancy and
drag in the water field, stiffness, contact with rock and coral, and paid out
from a reel. It can wrap around a pillar, snag on a branch, or loop on itself.

**A dive fails when the vehicle is held:** fouled, wrapped, or at full scope
and pulled back. The result says where and on what.

**Done when:** a route that circles the pillar twice ends in a fouled tether
and a failed dive, and the same route flown the other way round does not.

## 8. Sediment and the water's clarity

Thruster wash near the bottom lifts sand. The plume drifts in the water field
and settles. While it hangs, the camera sees less and a turbidity sensor reads
it.

**Done when:** a vehicle that skims the sand loses its own view behind it, and
the turbidity reading rises and falls with the plume.

## 9. One light clock

One clock drives the sun, or the LEDs in a tank, the scene's lighting, the
camera's exposure, the fish's day and the coral's polyps. A dive started at dusk
looks like dusk and behaves like dusk.

**Done when:** the same reef flown at noon and at 19:00 differs in light, in
which fish are out, and in what the camera counts.

## 10. The seabed around every place

- **GEBCO 2026** (public domain, 15 arc-seconds) gives the slope and deep water
  around each place. Its TID grid says, cell by cell, whether the depth was
  measured or interpolated.
- **The IHO DCDB** gives real multibeam where a ship has surveyed. That is
  likely parts of the Florida Keys, and patches of the Red Sea.

**Done when:** every place has its surroundings from GEBCO, and any place
covered by DCDB multibeam uses it, with each cell labelled by where it came from.

## 11. Towed platforms and the EdgeTech 2300

KAUST tows a 2300. To fly one we need a surface ship, a long cable (item 7 at
scale) and a towfish held at height by speed and cable out. On the towfish:

- tri-frequency side-scan as a waterfall image, with shadows behind anything
  raised
- the 2300B's bathymetry, on the multibeam we already have
- a sub-bottom profiler against sediment layers we would have to assume until
  KAUST gives us cores or old profiles

**Done when:** a tow line over a place produces a side-scan waterfall and a
sub-bottom section. The 2300's frequencies and ranges come from its brochure or
KAUST's own unit sheet, not from a guess.

## 12. Boxfish Luna

A hovering reef AUV, 25 kg, 730 × 435 × 351 mm, with 8 vectored thrusters.
Built for exactly the repeat reef survey the buyers want. It goes in the
catalogue as a reference vehicle:

- 4K and stereo cameras
- DVL and USBL
- Oculus imaging sonar
- CTD
- turbidity, chlorophyll and pH, read from the water and the sediment
- a hydrophone
- 600 Wh battery
- optional neutrally buoyant fibre tether

Thruster placement and hydrodynamic coefficients are assumed from photographs
and the hull's shape until Boxfish or KAUST gives us figures.

**Done when:** Luna flies a transect survey over a reef. Its stereo camera
reports a fish count, which is scored against the fish system's truth, and its
wash's effect on the coral is in the result.

## 13. The report that sells it

A dive's deliverable gains a section no other tool can write:

- what the vehicle did to the reef: colonies struck, sediment lifted, fish
  displaced
- how far its own presence biased what it counted

Those are the questions KCRI and the rest asked, answered before anyone gets
wet.

**Done when:** the same survey flown by mini-hoot and by Luna produces two
reports that differ in disturbance and in count bias, each with its provenance.
