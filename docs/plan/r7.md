# The seventh plan: work you can draw, fly and keep

*Proposed 4 October 2026, for approval. Nothing here is built yet.*

r6 made the ocean behave. The app has not kept up with it. A person who comes to plan a reef survey meets three screens — Missions, Dive and Sweeps — that each hold a third of one idea, and the map they would draw on is hidden under Places. r7 rebuilds that path around the work, and carries what r6 left open.

## What is there now, and why it confuses

| Screen | What it actually does | What is wrong with it |
|---|---|---|
| **Places → a place → Lay out** | The map editor: a plan view of the place over its seabed, where an arrangement is drawn — an array, nursery frames, a mooring — each thing landed on the bottom. | It is the only map in the app you can draw on, and it is reached three clicks deep under Places, not from the work it is for. |
| **Missions** | A mission is a place, an arrangement of it, and stages in order. Stages are picked from a list and point at drawn things by name. | No map: you cannot see or draw where a stage happens. You cannot open a mission to read it, edit it, or delete it — only create one and add stages. |
| **Dive** | Flies one thing once: a place, a vehicle, a task or a mission, the water, and who flies it. Watched live or not. | Fine for one run; it knows nothing of the doubts. |
| **Sweeps** | Flies one mission many times, once for every combination of the doubts ticked — the current, the positioning, the visibility, a failure, the array or mooring not where drawn, the length of the day — and ranks which doubt breaks it and which change saves the most. | It is a dive flown many times, with a different name and a different screen. Nobody can tell from the screens that a sweep is a dive. |

## The proposal

### 1. The mission designer — one page, one map

A mission opens on its place's map. The seabed, the reef, and the arrangement are drawn on it, and the stages are drawn on it too:

- **Stages on the map.** A waypoint route is drawn as clicked points. A survey is drawn as a rectangle or polygon, with its lanes shown as they will be flown. A transect is a line, and an inspection a ring round a drawn thing. Each stage shows its depth or altitude and its time.
- **The list beside it.** The stages in order, dragged to reorder, each opening its numbers.
- **The arrangement on the same map.** The layout editor moves here, as a layer. Drawing the array and drawing the survey over it are the same act on the same chart.
- **Time and battery.** "25 min of work" becomes a running total against the vehicle's battery and the day, stage by stage.

### 2. Missions you can keep

The library lists every mission with its place, its arrangement, and when it was last flown. From the library you can:

- **Open** a mission read-only.
- **Edit** it. An edit saves a new version, because runs pin a version and an old result must keep meaning what it meant.
- **Duplicate** it.
- **Delete** it — archived, not erased. It leaves the library, and the runs that flew it still say what they flew.

### 3. Fly — Dive and Sweeps become one page

One page flies a mission, or a single task. It asks the same questions it asks today, and adds one more: *what could go differently?*

- **Nothing ticked.** One run, watched live if wanted. This is today's dive.
- **Doubts ticked.** One run per combination, times the repeats. This is today's sweep, with the same estimate of runs and time before it starts.

The results page is one place for both: a run, or a set of runs with the ranking of what breaks the mission. Every run offers its film (`dives_film`) and its report: what it did to the reef, and what it missed.

### 4. What r6 left open, carried

- **Colony 16.** *Done 4 October:* a mission can chart `hazards`, and wary keeps clear of them before any ping hears them; no stony colony struck over eight seeds.
- **The four missing vehicle models.** *Done 4 October:* EdgeTech 2300, REMUS 100, Seaglider and BlueROV2 Heavy have hulls and pictures, published; the Heavy flies.
- **Control surfaces and gliding.** REMUS 100 steers with fins and Seaglider glides; the runtime only allocates thrusters, so both are drawn and not yet flyable.
- **The BlueROV2's thruster height.** Its horizontals are at z = +0.085 m, above the centre of gravity in this platform's z-up frame. That may be a sign carried over from a z-down source, or right for a vehicle whose centre of gravity is low; it needs checking against Wu (2018) before it is changed, because changing it changes how the most-flown vehicle behaves.
- **Per-panel hull forces** (r6 item 14's next round), checked against Li et al.'s force–speed curves, so rotational drag can be taken from the hull too.
- **The open-water flow grid.** A grid that moves with the vehicle, on Warp, so the wash outside a tank lingers as it does inside one.
- **Sediment erodibility.** Measured, not assumed — this needs a survey or a paper value for the place's sand.

## Done when

- A mission can be drawn on its map — route, survey area, inspection — saved, opened, edited into a new version, duplicated and archived, all in the app.
- The same page flies it once or under doubts, and the results of both are read in one place.
- The Places page no longer holds the only map you can draw on.
- The four vehicles have cards with pictures and fly in dives.

## Questions for you

1. **Delete or archive?** I recommend archive: the record of what was flown stays honest, and the library stays clean. A true delete is possible only for a mission that was never flown.
2. **Where should Fly start?** From a mission (the usual case), with "a single task" as the quick path. Or the other way round, as today.
3. **The arrangement.** Keep it pinned per mission (today: a mission flown in September sees the site as it was laid in March), or let a mission follow the latest layout?
