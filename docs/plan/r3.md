# The third plan: five places, one platform

What `r2.md` got structurally wrong, and this corrects: it made Looe Key a
*step*. Step 7 rebuilt Looe Key, step 8 built a library from what Looe Key
taught, and the other four places were swept up at the end in step 11. That is
not a plan for five places. It is a plan for one place with four afterthoughts,
and it happened because Looe Key is the only site anybody has ever fetched
evidence for.

So this plan separates two things the last one confused:

  **The machinery** — materials, assets, life, ground, the renderer. Built
  once, and every place gets it.

  **The evidence** — what each place is actually made of. Per place, and
  nobody else's.

Everything still owed from `r2.md` is carried forward here, with its status.
Nothing is dropped and nothing is quietly restarted.

## Where the five places actually stand

The numbers that forced this rewrite:

| | ground | colonies | cover checked? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **looe-key** | NOAA lidar, 2 m, 8 surveys | 83,055 / km² | **yes** — CREMP, September |
| **kaneohe** | NOAA topobathy lidar, 3.4 m | 500,000 / km² | no |
| **al-fahal** | Sentinel-2 derived, 5.9 m | 44,000 / km² | no |
| **red-sea** | composed | **1,400,000 / km²** | no — and it is composed |
| **thuwal-deep** | GMRT synthesis, 15.7 m | 0 | correct: it is 520 m down |
| **mhl-tank** | a published tank | none | it is a tank |

**Looe Key has seventeen times fewer colonies per square kilometre than Red
Sea**, because Looe Key is the only place where somebody went and looked. In
September a reef claiming 86% stony cover met a survey saying 3.4% and was
rebuilt. The other three still carry the number that was thrown out.

That is not an aesthetic problem. It is the same honesty problem this project
has had before, and a render of a reef seventeen times too dense is a picture
that will be cited.

**And only Looe Key has a reference corpus at all.** One directory, 186 MB:
bathymetry, habitat polygons, CREMP tables, 47 photographs, species counts, and
one Smithsonian coral scan. The other four have a published package and nothing
behind it. The USGS photogrammetry for Looe Key — the 5 mm orthomosaic, the
1 cm DEM, the point cloud — is catalogued with its DOI and **not fetched: 52
files, 97 GB.**

## The rule, carried forward unchanged

**No step is done until it has a screen.** It held for the last plan and it
caught step 18 half-finished. It holds here.

**And nothing invented is allowed in.** Colonies from scans, cover from
surveys, water from measured coefficients, weather from a service that measured
it. Where a number cannot be sourced it is a parameter with its uncertainty
beside it. The colony counts above are what happens when this slips.

# Phase A · The machinery every place gets

Built once. Judged on all five, not on whichever one looks best.

## A1 · Colonies that were scanned

There is one real coral in this repository: `usnm_58_orbicella_coronata.glb`,
a Smithsonian CC0 type specimen, 100,000 triangles with UVs, normals and three
texture maps. It has been sitting in Looe Key's reference since September,
unused, while every colony in every frame is a procedural blob.

  **Use it**, as the mounding prototype. *Orbicella* is the dominant
  reef-builder at Looe Key and a mounding coral is the commonest thing in
  frame everywhere.

  **Fetch the rest.** The Smithsonian's 3D API is CC0 and needs no account;
  the reference tool already proves the path. They are dry skeletons, so they
  give form and not colour — the colour comes from the photographs each place
  has, which is a per-place question and belongs in Phase B.

  **And keep the procedural ones** for what scans cannot give: the long tail
  of shapes nobody has scanned, and the variation within a species that a
  single specimen cannot express.

**Screen:** a place's page shows what its colonies are made of, and which are
scans.

**Done:** a frame with the water taken away shows coral rather than lumps.

### Where A1 got to

**In.** `tools/scanned.py` reads a Smithsonian glb; `tools/scans` fetches more
of them from the Open Access API by growth form. Looe Key's reef is built by
`tools/reef-survey` off the USGS colony list, not by `tools/reef.py`, and that
is where the scan is wired: 21 prototypes where there were 15, six of them the
scan, **8,888 of 83,055 colonies**. Both paths carry it.

Three things the first attempt got wrong, all of them now recorded in the code
that was wrong:

  The simplifier kept the largest triangles. The largest triangles on a scan
  are the flat ones, so it kept a slab and threw away half the depth of the
  specimen. Vertex clustering instead, which is uniform.

  The specimen is a third museum plinth by height. Sat on its lowest point it
  rendered as a coral standing on a plinth. `where_the_ground_is` finds the
  step and buries everything under it.

  Fifteen prototypes were five shapes in three colours, because the geometry
  was grown once per kind and reused across the shades. Every prototype gets
  its own draw now.

**Fetched.** With a key in `SI_API_KEY`, `tools/scans` pulled **eleven
specimens over five growth forms**, CC0, scale in metres:

| form | specimens |
| --- | --- |
| massive | *Astraea (Orbicella) coronata*, *Porites lobata*, *Porites monticulosa* |
| brain | *Diploria labyrinthiformis*, *Pseudodiploria strigosa* |
| branching | *Pocillopora molokensis*, *Madrepora horrida*, *Sideropora mordax* |
| table | *Leptoseris cailletti*, *Gemmipora brassica* |
| encrusting | *Millepora alcicornis* |

**No gorgonians.** The Smithsonian has no CC0 3D scan of a sea fan or a sea
plume, and those are the silhouette of this reef. They stay procedural and the
record says why.

Three things the fetcher got wrong first, each now in the code that was wrong:

  **The same specimen came back under several genera** through synonymy, so
  USNM 58 was downloaded three times under three names. Deduplicated by the
  package it came from, not by the name it was given. A reef standing three
  copies of one museum piece is worse than one standing a blob, because it
  reads as variation.

  **Files were named from the genus that was searched for**, which turned
  *Madrepora horrida* into `acropora_madrepora`: an identification nobody made.
  Named from the museum's own title now, and the growth form lives in
  `scans.json` beside it rather than in the filename.

  **`found_in` returned one scan per form.** Three different Acropora on a reef
  is the point; one Acropora three times is not.

`scanned.found_in` now reads `scans.json` where there is one and falls back to
the genus in the filename for a specimen somebody put there by hand. 21 tests
in `tools/`, and the six that used to skip because they named a file that had
been renamed now find the specimen the way the code finds it.

## A2 · Materials that are not `UsdPreviewSurface`

Carried from r2 step 6, which is part done: roughness, self-emission and
subdivision are in, and the three things that matter most are not, because the
preview surface cannot express them.

  **Subsurface scattering** — translucent tissue over white aragonite, which
  is why living coral glows and bleached coral does not.
  **Fluorescence** — GFP-like proteins re-emitting green and orange under
  blue-shifted light, and most of why reef footage looks otherworldly.
  **Displacement** at corallite scale.

All three need an MDL material. That is the step.

**Done:** the same colony under the same light, before and after, and the
second one is alive.

### Where A2 got to

**`catalog/materials/coral_tissue.mdl`** is in and rendering. Translucent
tissue over white aragonite, a wet sheen, light through the thin parts, an
`alive` parameter that takes a colony from healthy to bare skeleton, and a
stand-in for fluorescence.

**`tools/corallite.py`** makes the surface at polyp scale, because there is no
CC0 photograph of one to tile: a jittered hexagonal packing of cups for the
mounding corals, meandering valleys for the brains, with corallite sizes from
the genera that build these reefs. Tileable, seeds wrapped across the eight
neighbouring copies, and the normals wrapped too. 13 tests. Projected triplanar
in world space, because the prototypes carry no texture coordinates and a blob
has no good unwrapping, and because world space means a corallite is 2.6 mm on
a colony of any size.

Three things it got wrong, each now in the code that was wrong:

  **`material()` has no `emission` parameter.** Emission belongs to
  `material_surface`. The compiler says "no overloaded version of function
  'material' matches calling parameters" and names the line, not the argument.

  **Diffuse transmission on a boulder is not translucency.** On a closed solid
  it is light going in and the bright sand behind coming out, and an olive
  colony rendered as a pale grey pebble — the bleached look this material
  exists to stop. It is per growth form now: nought for a boulder, which is
  thirty centimetres of aragonite, and a real value for a branch tip or the
  edge of a plate.

  **The subsurface term mixed the skeleton's colour in directly.** Light
  scattering a millimetre down under tissue brightens what comes back; it does
  not wash it towards white.

**Left:** the colonies still read paler than the reef they are on, and that is
**not** the material. The preview surface rendered the same colony at 0.587
against an albedo of 0.26, so it predates all of this: the light on an
upward-facing surface is hot, which is the sun-and-dome balance, and that is
A5. Displacement at corallite scale is a bump and not displacement, and says so.

## A3 · Ground that holds at a metre

Carried from r2 step 10, not started, and the most stubborn thing in the last
two days. Detail maps are tiled, grain is baked in, anisotropic filtering is
on, and the floor is still the flattest thing in frame at close range.

  Habitat-class detail sets, **height-blended** rather than mixed.
  Sand ripples as real displacement, oriented by the current.
  The satellite colour demoted to a low-frequency tint.

**Done:** a frame from a vehicle sitting on the bottom, on sand, nowhere near a
colony, that is worth looking at.

### Where A3 got to

The reason the floor was flat was not the detail maps. It was that **none of
the ground a place has a record of was ever drawn.** `tools/ground` has been
building Looe Key's real seabed since the second of September: the USGS
orthomosaic where the survey flew, habitat class from the satellite's pattern
elsewhere, and a 1 m heightfield with the survey's own DEM feathered into
NOAA's two. The renderer opened a 2 m mesh with a stock beach tiled over it.

Three things changed, and all three show:

  **The terrain is the 1 m field.** Two million triangles where there were half
  a million, and relief under five metres goes from 0.18 m to 0.52 m at p99.
  That is the spur-and-groove, and it is the survey's, not an invention.

  **`catalog/materials/coral_ground.mdl`**, because no material in OpenUSD can
  multiply a map of one particular seabed by a tiled photograph of a real
  surface, and a seabed needs both: the site map is 24 cm a texel and smears at
  close range, the tiled photograph is the same few metres of somebody else's
  beach everywhere. Multiplied, with the tile divided through by its own
  measured average, the tile carries variation and no colour and the site map
  carries colour and no detail. A place still carries a preview surface for
  anything that cannot read MDL.

  **Two surfaces, height-blended by habitat class.** `damp_beach_sand_02` in
  the grooves and sediment, `coral_ground_02` on the spurs and pavement, chosen
  by the polygons the survey drew. Blended by height, so at the edge of a sand
  patch the pavement shows through the gaps between the grains instead of the
  two fading through a grey that is neither. The seabed was `coast_sand_03`,
  which is a shingle bank in Normandy.

**Left:** the frame is still dim and brown where a carbonate reef is pale, and
that is exposure, which is A5. Sand ripples oriented by the current are not
done, though the sand now has ripples of its own.

## A4 · It moves, and something lives in it

Carried from r2 step 9, not started. Gorgonians and soft coral flexing in the
current that is already in the physics. Then fish: schooling, per-species
behaviour, and scatter-and-reform when a thruster comes past, which is the part
an ROV pilot recognises instantly.

Placed by measured density and habitat class, so a monitoring mission can be
**scored** on counting them rather than swimming past scenery.

**Done:** ten seconds where nothing is being demonstrated and it is still worth
watching.

## A5 · The rest of the light

Carried from r2 steps 2, 3 and 5, all part done.

  **Red must die faster than green.** The water model is spectral and the
  renderer's fog takes one distance, so a red vehicle at fifteen metres still
  photographs the same as a blue one. Needs a medium the renderer does not
  have, or the frame's own depth.
  **Caustics from the wave spectrum** that Phase 2 of the last plan built, and
  volumetric shafts.
  **A camera that meters the frame** rather than computing exposure from
  depth. This is why a lamp correct at six hundred metres blows out at six.
  **Backscatter and marine snow**, which is the defining look of ROV footage.

**Done:** no frame in the sheet is blown out or crushed, and the surface stops
being a bright ceiling with hard edges.

### Where A5 got to: the camera meters the frame

**`/rtx/post/tonemap/iso` does not exist. Neither does `cameraShutter`.**

carb takes any key you hand it, so writing to a name the renderer does not
have creates a setting nothing reads, silently. The exposure has been written
to those two names for a fortnight. A **thirty-two fold** change in "iso"
moved the picture by a thousandth of a stop, which is how it was finally
caught: pin the exposure, render, measure the frame, and the two frames are the
same. Every argument about the lamps in that fortnight was an argument against
a control that was not connected to anything.

The real names are `filmIso` and `exposureTime`. `CORAL_CITY_SETTINGS=1` now
prints the renderer's whole `/rtx` tree, and it has to be asked **from the
frame loop**: before the renderer starts, every key under `/rtx` is one this
code invented, which is exactly why the dump taken at dive open showed only our
own five made-up settings and looked fine.

On top of that, `coral/metering.py`: meter the frame, three or four rounds,
then stop. Not the renderer's automatic exposure, which tracks for ever and
brightens a dark scene until it looks normal — that destroys the thing a dive
is measuring. A diver meters on the way down and shoots, and the picture gets
darker as they go deeper, which is true. 16 tests, no renderer in them.

Three things it got wrong on the way, each one now in the code that was wrong:

  **`schedule_capture(ByteCapture(...))` delivered exactly one frame per run**
  and then stopped, with no error. Metering now goes through
  `capture_viewport_to_file`, which is the call the sheet itself is made with
  and is therefore known to work here.

  **Every budget was in wall-clock seconds**, and a stills tour over two
  million triangles gets four updates in forty seconds while the scene
  resolves. The meter timed out mid-first-round. It counts updates now. The
  same mistake in the other direction ate the budget once the tour was held
  and updates became free.

  **The highlight guard only applied once the frame was already clipping**,
  which is not a guard. A bright end at 0.78 was read as headroom, the
  exposure was raised for the mid-tones, and every view in the sheet came back
  with its top half-percent at pure white.

  **The meter read a bilinear copy of the frame.** It reads a smaller copy
  because it runs on the frame loop, and averaging four pixels into one throws
  the bright tail away: on one frame it reported a bright end of 0.85 where
  the number was 0.92. A percentile of an averaged image is not the percentile
  of the image, so the meter believed it had a third of a stop of headroom it
  did not have and every sheet came back hot. Point sampling now.

Measured over the five views of the sheet, middle of frame and bright end and
what fraction is pure white:

# Phase B · Each place gets its evidence

One block per place. The order is by what each one is *for*, not by which is
easiest.

## B1 · Looe Key, the calibration

The only site with ground truth at the scale of a camera frame, and therefore
the only place where "is this right" can be answered rather than argued.

  **Fetch the USGS photogrammetry.** 52 files, 97 GB, public domain, DOI
  10.5066/P1QRS3SK. This is a real download and a real processing job and it
  is not an afternoon.
  **The ground becomes the 1 cm DEM**, with the metre-scale spur relief the
  2 m bathymetry rounds off.
  **The colour becomes the 5 mm orthomosaic.**
  **The colonies become the colonies** — segment the orthomosaic and each
  patch is a coral somebody photographed.

**Done:** a render of a named square metre beside the USGS photograph of that
square metre, and the difference is the water, not the reef.

## B2 · The three places whose cover nobody checked

**This comes before any of them is made prettier.** Red Sea at 1.4 million
colonies a square kilometre, Kaneohe at half a million, Al Fahal at 44
thousand: three different densities, none of them measured, all of them
rendered as fact.

  **Kaneohe** — Hawai'i has long-term monitoring and the Allen Coral Atlas
  covers the bay. There is a number to be found.
  **Al Fahal** — KAUST has surveyed this reef. Somebody has counted it.
  **Red Sea** — composed, so there is nothing to check it against, and that
  is the answer: it is not a reef anybody can cite, its cover should be
  whatever a person chose *on purpose*, and its page should say so.

**Done:** every place's colony count traces to a survey or is labelled as
chosen, and no page shows an unsourced density as though it were measured.

## B3 · Al Fahal, the one everything is flown in

Sixty-seven of the sixty-eight dives in the record are here, so it is the site
that most needs to be right and the one with the least behind it: satellite
bathymetry at six metres a sample, and no reference corpus at all.

  A reference fetch of its own: what is known about this reef, from whoever
  measured it.
  Indo-Pacific species rather than the Caribbean library.
  ICESat-2 for calibrated depths, which needs a free NASA Earthdata account
  and is **not ours to create**.

## B4 · Kaneohe

The best-looking of the five already, and the geometry is good lidar. What it
needs is its cover checked and Hawaiian species.

## B5 · Thuwal Deep, a different world

Six hundred metres: no sunlight, no coral, no algae, and none of Phase A's
library applies. Everything is seen by lamp, which is built. What is owed is
soft bioturbated sediment with tracks and burrows, whip corals, glass sponges,
holothurians, brine pools, and marine snow as the atmosphere.

And a mission, because it has never had one.

## B6 · The tank

Not a reef and not to be made one. It is the only place on this platform whose
geometry is *known* rather than derived, which makes it the home for the
physics validation in C7.

# Phase C · Carried forward from the second plan

Everything in `r2.md` that is not aesthetic. Status is as of 17 September.

## C1 · A dive is a real dive — *part done*

**Done:** a mission says where it launches, with a picker; the closing
navigation fix; a day that pays for getting there and back; a JONSWAP sea state
acting on the vehicle; an acoustic modem that `asking` goes through.

**Owed:** descent with DVL bottom-lock, the transit and run-in legs, recovery.
And weather *history* — "fly it as it was last Tuesday" — rather than only what
the sea is doing now.

## C2 · The vehicle is yours — *part done*

**Done:** the sonar reaches a deployed stack as a `LaserScan`; a CTD that reads
the water and brings a profile home; every instrument and the computer drawing
real power, with a screen to fit them on; the SDK's third command form and a
glider controller anybody outside can write.

**Owed:** multibeam and side-scan, which are the instruments a survey AUV is
actually bought for. Publishing your own vehicle from the SDK. The Heavy and
the REMUS, which are written and unpublished.

## C3 · It can be trusted — *not started*

**The physics held against something real.** Fossen 6-DOF, properly
implemented, and not one coefficient checked against a real vehicle. The tank
is the place; a towed hull at a stated speed is a drag measurement.

**Something comes out of it.** A score and a video is not a deliverable.
Outplant positions as GeoJSON, a colony inventory as CSV, coverage as a
georeferenced raster. The georeference exists and is exactly determined.

**Missions that are plans of work.** Three of the four on the platform are
parametric or are test fixtures.

## C4 · It can be judged — *not started*

Four controllers that have never met, and the Autonomy screen that is still a
deploy log answering a question nobody asks.

# How this one is kept honest

**Judged on five, not on one.** Every Phase A step is checked with
`tools/look` across all five places in the same run. A change that improves
Looe Key and ruins Kaneohe is not an improvement, and the last plan had no way
of noticing.

**Evidence before polish.** B2 comes before anything in Phase B is made
prettier, because a beautiful render of a reef seventeen times too dense is
worse than an ugly one — it is more likely to be believed.

**The instrument is not optional.** Four bugs in the test rig cost more of the
last two days than the rendering did: a stale image, an empty environment
variable, a light placed out of frame, and frames crossing between places.
Every frame now carries the build and the place that made it and both are
checked before it is kept. That machinery stays and gets extended, not
bypassed.

**Run the product's tests, not only the platform's.** The SDK's suite had been
failing for at least a hundred commits and nobody had run it. The thing an
outside developer touches first is the thing least tested.

**What would show this plan is wrong:** the USGS fetch in B1 turning out to be
unusable at the scale it claims; the cover checks in B2 finding no survey
exists for Kaneohe or Al Fahal, which would make three of five places
permanently uncitable; or all of Phase A landing and a reef scientist still
picking the render out of a line-up immediately.
