# The forum: Saudi Maritime & Logistics Congress, 21–22 October 2026

*Agreed 5 October 2026.* Jeddah Superdome; about 15,000 attendees. The user attends as a visitor and takes meetings.

## Who is there

- **Ports:** Mawani, King Abdullah Port, Saudi Global Ports.
- **Shipping:** Bahri (founding partner), Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM.
- **Offshore marine services:** Zamil Offshore, Gulf Marine Services, Jana Marine.
- **Aramco**, the main partner.
- **ABS**, the classification society.

KAUST, KCRI and Red Sea Global may come as visitors. It is not their event.

## Exhibitors to meet (from the exhibitor list, docs/summit.html)

- **Ocean-tech makers: Kongsberg Discovery, Kongsberg Maritime, Exail, Sercel.** Partners as much as clients. Their AUVs, sonars and navigation are what a package describes, and the platform flies them in Saudi places with provenance. They have simulators of their own, so the offer is their vehicle in real Saudi water, not a simulator.
- **Pipelines and offshore: Saudi Aramco, TAQA, NDT Global, NDT Corrosion Control, Zamil Offshore, International Marine Construction, Jana Marine, Ampelmann.** The pipeline example.
- **Dredging and ports: Saudi Dredging Company, Red Sea Gateway Terminal, Saudi Global Ports, National Ports Marine Services, Ecocoast.** The dredging-plume example. Saudi Dredging is the exact fit.
- **Classification: DNV, Lloyd's Register** (and ABS among the speakers). The provenance example.
- **Academic and government: King Abdulaziz University (the Almobdioon stand), National Maritime Academy, Innovation Norway and the Norwegian embassy.**

## What we bring

One generic deck, in English, saying what the platform is and that it is tuned to the visitor's case. Each kind of visitor gets an example shown where it fits. A one-page handout, and the films on a tablet.

## The examples, each a place, a dive, its numbers and a film

1. **Offshore: a pipeline corridor inspected.** A pipeline laid across the Red Sea place's fringing reef, sand terrace and slope, as a landfall would be. It is surveyed by an ROV following it and by a towed side-scan along it. Coverage, time, energy and what the vehicle did to the reef beside it.
2. **Ports: a berth beside a reef.** A dredged basin and channel next to a fringing reef, with a quay wall. A dredging plume is carried by the current onto the colonies, measured as settled sediment against Erftemeijer's thresholds. A quay-wall inspection is planned and flown.
3. **Reef: Shushah outplant and survey planning.** This exists: the place is fixed and published, and the designer draws the missions.
4. **Class: a run's provenance.** This exists. Every number in a result says measured, derived, chosen or assumed, and where it came from.
5. **Makers: bring your vehicle.** A package (hull, thrusters, sensors, battery) made from a maker's published sheet and flown in a Saudi place. The four vehicles drawn this month (REMUS 100, Seaglider, BlueROV2 Heavy, EdgeTech 2300) are the evidence of how fast that is.

## Progress

- **Offshore example, flown 5 October.** `examples/pipeline-landfall.json`, made with `tools/example`. A 452 m line on the Red Sea place, from the fore-reef at 7.5 m out to 34 m; the seabed under it gives it three free spans, of 1, 4 and 7 m. REMUS 100 followed it at 4.5 m, on line-of-sight guidance and a USBL fix, in 308 s. It saw all 452 m and all three spans, and struck none of the 1.1 million colonies. Getting there fixed four things that would have shown in front of a client:
  - a launch point that was never applied;
  - a REMUS that looped round points it could not turn onto;
  - a depth controller that wound up diving from the surface and drove into the coral;
  - launch points drawn in the app that the runtime could not read.
