# The second plan, end to end

The first plan built a loop: lay out a site, plan work over it, ask what breaks
it, get an answer. All nineteen steps are flown and in the record, and the loop
works.

This plan is about the loop being **true** and being **seen**. Everything below
came out of one afternoon of looking at what is actually there:

  Five places, and four of them photograph badly enough to embarrass you.
  Four missions, and one of them is a real plan of work.
  No mission says where it starts, so the reef picks the spot by coral cover.
  Physics of the right shape, with coefficients nobody has ever checked.
  No fish, no life, no sensors anything consumes, no weather.
  A vehicle you cannot fit, customise, or bring your own of.
  Four controllers that have never met.

None of that is a missing feature. It is the difference between a thing that
demonstrates and a thing somebody uses.

---

## The rule that runs through all of it

**No step is done until it has a screen.**

The first plan learned this the expensive way: the interface was listed as item
63, "built as each item lands, or it becomes a second project that never
starts" — and it half-happened, so today the terminal can do a dozen things the
application cannot. Every step below names the screen it needs, and a step
whose machinery works and whose screen does not is not finished.

**And the second rule, from the same afternoon:** nothing invented is allowed
in. Colonies come from scans, cover from surveys, water from measured
coefficients, weather from a weather service, vehicle coefficients from a trial.
Where a number cannot be sourced it is a parameter with its uncertainty written
beside it, not a value somebody liked. This was bought the hard way in
September when a reef claiming 86% cover met a survey saying 3.4%.

# Phase 1 · It looks like the sea

Four of five places are unusable as pictures. The renderer is not the problem —
it is Isaac's RTX path tracer, the same class of thing that renders film
previs. The problem is that **there is no optical water model at all**
(`hydrodynamics.py` computes density for buoyancy and its own header admits
absorption and backscatter are "the hard part" and skips them), and **every
colony is one flat colour** (`UsdPreviewSurface`, one RGB triple, no maps, no
UVs, `subdivisionScheme = "none"`).

Ground and geometry are fine. Everything between the ground and the camera is
missing.

## 1 · A frame you can compare

`tools/look`. Named viewpoints per place — at the work site, on the bottom
looking along it, mid-water looking down, one at the site edge — rendered
identically everywhere, with the parameters that made them stamped in the
corner.

Before anything changes, because every step in this phase is a judgement about
an image and a judgement made from memory is not a judgement. `tools/fly-over`
flies a path and hands back whatever it passed; two runs are not comparable.

**Screen:** the sheet itself, and a place's own page showing its newest frames.

**Done:** the same command twice, a week apart, differs only where somebody
changed something.

*Done (15 September).* `tools/look`, and a `Stills` mode in the runtime beside
`Tour` and `Ladder`. Four named views, ninety seconds a place, the frames
written under their own names with what made them beside them.

It paid for itself before it was used for anything. Three bugs, all of which
had been read as something else:

  **Looking down at a reef from twelve metres came back an empty rectangle**,
  and the obvious reading was that the water was too thick. It was not. The
  floor at Al Fahal is nine metres down, so twelve metres above it is three
  metres into the *air*, and a water surface from that side is opaque. A view
  now says how far off the bottom it wants to be and the water says how far off
  the bottom there is; in shallow water the water wins.

  **Straight down with up as z is a degenerate basis** and `SetLookAt` hands
  back nonsense for it. Near-vertical views take north as up instead.

  **The tour could not have found either**, because it flies a path and hands
  back whatever it passed. Six frames of a place are not six views of it.

## 2 · Water that absorbs and scatters

`coral/optics.py`. Absorption `a(λ)` and scattering `b(λ)` kept apart, because
they do opposite things and one fog density conflates them — which is how the
current frames manage to have no contrast *and* no glow.

  **Spectral.** Red runs about 0.4 per metre in clear ocean and blue about
  0.02. That twentyfold ratio *is* the underwater look.

  **Jerlov types** — oceanic I–III, coastal 1C–9C — with measured
  coefficients, so "clear Red Sea" is a selection and not an adjustment.

  **Henyey–Greenstein, g ≈ 0.92.** Seawater scatters hard forward. The veiling
  glare around anything bright is the most recognisable cue there is.

  **Driven by what a reef programme measures** — chlorophyll-a, CDOM,
  suspended sediment — so the aesthetic and the science are one system.

**Screen:** water type on the dive composer, beside the current, with a
thumbnail of what it looks like.

**Done:** the same reef at Jerlov I and 5C, and the difference is one a diver
would describe.

*Half done, and the half that is left is not the half this step expected
(15 September).*

What was true: `coral/water.py` already held the right idea and the right
numbers — `ATTENUATION_METRES = (4, 17, 26)`, Jerlov 1C, with the physics
written out above it. And **one third of it reached the picture**. The
renderer's fog takes a single distance, so green was used for how dark it gets
with depth and red and blue were computed and thrown away — which is why a red
vehicle at fifteen metres came out the same colour as a blue one.

What is built now: the ten Jerlov types as a table; a **veiling colour derived
from them** rather than picked, so the distance goes blue in clear ocean and
green-brown in a turbid bay without anybody choosing either; the fog's three
knobs derived from the water instead of tuned by eye against one horizontal
view; and the type named by the conditions, so `WATER=II ./tools/look al-fahal`
is a thing somebody can ask.

**And then the frames came back and the premise of this phase did not
survive.** The same reef through clear ocean and through clear coastal water
differs by a hue shift and nothing else, because the fog was never what was
wrong with these pictures. What dominates a frame is: a ground slab with no
texture on it at any range a camera works at, colonies with one flat colour
each, a hard seam where the surface meets the water, and an exposure balance
that leaves the foreground dark and the distance bright.

So the ordering rule at the bottom of this plan — *water before assets, not
negotiable* — is **wrong as stated**, and it was written from the tour's frames
rather than from a comparison. The water work was still worth doing and had to
happen: the medium is now a declared thing derived from measured coefficients
rather than eight numbers somebody tuned, and nothing about materials can be
judged through a medium nobody can state. But it is a prerequisite, not the
lever. **The lever is the ground and the colonies**, and the frames say so.

What is still owed here, and it is the part the fog cannot do: red really does
die four times faster than green over the same path, and a single fog distance
cannot say so. That needs either a medium the renderer does not have, or the
frame's own depth — and the viewport capture hands back colour without it.
Both are real options and neither is this step.

## 3 · The surface, and the light through it

Caustics animated from the wave spectrum the physics will grow in step 13 —
not a looping texture, because a loop is a lie a scientist spots in four
seconds. Volumetric shafts, which fall out of a real medium. Snell's window.
Sun by latitude, time of day and depth, split from skylight.

**Screen:** time of day on the composer, and the frame changes.

**Done:** three metres at noon and fifteen at four o'clock, and nobody has to
be told which is which.

## 4 · Lamps, and what they light

Photometric cones from the vehicle's own package, and **backscatter** — light
hitting particulate right in front of the port, which is the defining look of
every piece of ROV footage ever shot. Marine snow at a density the water type
sets.

This is the step that makes six hundred metres possible at all.

**Screen:** lamps in the vehicle fit (step 18), on and off, with their draw.

**Done:** Thuwal Deep stops being a black rectangle.

## 5 · The camera is a camera

Physically-based sensor with **auto-exposure**, because an ROV camera has one
and the way it hunts is half of why real footage looks the way it does. Dome
port distortion, chromatic aberration, vignette, ACES, a grade.

Here rather than first: an auto-exposure over a wrong medium tunes itself to
the wrong thing, and some of Al Fahal's washed-out cast is certainly this and
cannot be told apart from the water until the water is right.

**Done:** no frame in the sheet is blown out or crushed, anywhere.

*Part done (16 September).* The exposure is still computed from depth by a
formula rather than metered off the frame, and that formula was hot at the top
of the column and a stop under at the bottom: a tenth of the shallow reef was
blowing to white while the deep site was too dark. Both moved.

The formula itself is the thing still owed, and it is not cosmetic. Because the
deep frame is metered several times more sensitively than the shallow one, a
lamp correct at six hundred metres is wrong at six — so the lamp's brightness
is a compromise between two depths rather than a photometric conversion.
Metering off what is in front of the lens fixes the camera and the lamp
together.

## 6 · Coral is tissue over skeleton

Materials, on the geometry that exists.

  **Subsurface scattering.** Translucent tissue over white aragonite. This is
  why living coral glows and bleached coral does not, and a diffuse surface
  physically cannot produce it. It is the main reason Al Fahal's colonies read
  as dead.

  **Fluorescence.** GFP-like proteins re-emit green and orange under
  blue-shifted light. It is most of why reef footage looks otherworldly, and
  it is measurable, so it is allowed in.

  **The rest of PBR** — albedo, roughness, normal, AO, displacement at
  corallite scale.

**Done:** one colony, same light, before and after, and the second is alive.

## 7 · Looe Key is rebuilt from the photograph

`tools/from-the-orthomosaic`. **The centre of this phase.**

The USGS flew a five-camera sled over the fore reef in July 2022 and published,
public domain: an 850 × 160 m orthomosaic at **5 mm**, a 1 cm DEM, and a
**10.5 billion point cloud**, covering the spur-and-groove from 4 m to 12 m
straight through the site centre. It is already fetched by `tools/reference`
and nothing has ever been built from it.

So the reef is not modelled. It is rebuilt: the ground is the DEM, the colour
is the orthomosaic, and **the colonies are the colonies** — segment it and
every patch is a coral somebody photographed, on a reef where CREMP already
says 2.7 *Siderastrea* and 1 *Porites* a square metre.

**Screen:** the place's page shows the render beside the photograph.

**Done:** a render of a named square metre beside the USGS photograph of that
square metre, and the difference is the water, not the reef.

## 8 · The library, from what Looe Key taught

Taught by a measured reef rather than by taste. The four stony species the
survey found — *Orbicella*, *Porites astreoides*, *Siderastrea siderea*,
*Montastraea cavernosa* — plus *Millepora* on the crest. Geometry from the
Smithsonian's CC0 type specimens, which are dry skeletons and give form;
tissue and colour from the orthomosaic and the 87 licensed photographs.

**And the octocorals, which are missing entirely.** *Gorgonia ventalina* fans a
metre across, *Antillogorgia* plumes and rods. Eighty of the reference
photographs are of these. They are the silhouette of a Caribbean reef and
there is not one in the model.

Then sponges, *Palythoa* mats, and **the six tenths of every frame that is not
coral at all** — pavement and low rock under turf algae. Getting the bare right
matters more than getting the coral right, because it is most of the picture.

**Done:** a frame of reef flat with no coral in it that still reads as the
Florida Keys.

## 9 · It moves, and something lives in it

Gorgonians and soft coral flex in the current — and the current is already in
the physics, so this is a connection rather than an animation.

Then **fish**, of which there are currently none, not even a stub. Boids for
schooling, per-species behaviour (a damselfish holds a patch, grunts hover in
the lee of a spur, a parrotfish grazes, a barracuda tracks the vehicle), and
**scatter-and-reform when a thruster comes past**, which is the part an ROV
pilot recognises instantly.

Placed by measured density and habitat class, not scattered — because fish
biomass is a thing reef programmes count, and placed properly they become
something a monitoring mission can be **scored on** rather than scenery.

**Done:** ten seconds where nothing is being demonstrated and it is still worth
watching.

## 10 · Ground as fine as the camera

Where there is no orthomosaic — four of the five — habitat-class detail
textures at several scales, height-blended rather than mixed, sand ripples as
real displacement oriented by the current, satellite colour demoted to a
low-frequency tint. At half a metre of altitude a 1 m satellite pixel is the
smear in the Al Fahal frames, and no amount of coral fixes it.

**Done:** a frame from the bottom, on sand, nowhere near a colony, worth
looking at.

## 11 · The other four places

**Al Fahal** — the library placed at the cover its evidence supports,
Indo-Pacific species, Jerlov oceanic. The water-box seam and the visible edge
of the world go. **And settled here rather than carried: `red-sea` and
`al-fahal` may be the same place twice.** If the first is a generic built
before the second was measured, retire it rather than dress it.

**Kaneohe** — patch reefs standing on sand in a lagoon, not a fringing slope.
Hawaiian species, turbid lagoon water. The lidar is good, so the geometry is
already there.

**Thuwal Deep** — a different world. Six hundred metres: no sunlight, no coral,
no algae, and none of the library applies. Everything is seen by lamp, so step
4 is the whole of the look. Soft bioturbated sediment with tracks and burrows,
whip corals, glass sponges, holothurians, the brine pools that make the basin
worth a mission at all, and marine snow as the atmosphere rather than an
effect.

**Done:** twenty frames, five places, four views, and it goes in front of
somebody at KAUST without an apology.

# Phase 2 · A dive is a real dive

A mission currently begins with the vehicle already at the work site, at
working depth, navigation zeroed. Real ones do not, and the difference is not
cosmetic: it is most of the energy, most of the clock, and all of the
navigation error.

## 12 · A mission says where it starts

A mission document has no start point. The site picks the spot — and picks it
by coral cover, from the discredited grown-reef numbers:

    spawned {"at": [854.2, -1106.7, -6.3],
             "why": "the best reef between eight and eighteen metres (94% cover at 9 m)"}

You can draw a ship and a dock and a mission cannot start at either. So:
**launch, descent, transit, run-in, recovery**, as the stages of a dive that
real AUV operations actually have.

  **Launch** from something drawn — a ship, a dock, a point. The surface fix
  that zeroes the navigation happens there, and everything after is dead
  reckoning from that instant.

  **Descent** as a profile, not a teleport, with **DVL bottom-lock
  acquisition** — above the DVL's range there is no bottom track and the
  vehicle is reckoning on nothing. A real mission does not start the survey
  until lock is confirmed.

  **Transit** to the first waypoint, because work rarely starts where you
  launch.

  **Run-in** — a survey line does not begin at the line. Fifty to two hundred
  metres of lead-in so the vehicle is settled on heading, altitude and speed
  before data counts.

  **Recovery**, which mirrors the launch and ends with a surface fix.

**Screen:** the mission builder gets a launch point picked off the layout, and
the stage list shows the transit and descent it implies.

**Done:** a mission that starts at the ship, descends, transits, runs in, works,
and comes back — and the record says how long each of those took.

## 13 · The closing fix, which is the honest score

Surface at the end, take a GPS fix, compare it to where the vehicle thought it
was. That single number is how the industry judges a dive's navigation, it is
free here because the simulator knows both, and it is not recorded.

It also makes the cost model honest: **"53 runs a day, held back by the
battery" counts work time only** — no transit, no descent, no recovery. That
number is optimistic and nobody knows by how much.

**Screen:** on the run page, beside the score: where it thought it was, where
it was, and the drift.

**Done:** every dive reports its closing error, and the cost sentence includes
getting there and back.

## 14 · Weather that was measured

The platform already distinguishes **constructed** conditions — where naming
an instant is *refused*, because inventing one claims a provenance you do not
have — from **observed** conditions, where an instant is *required*. That
distinction is built, enforced and tested, and **nothing has ever created an
observed condition.** The slot is cut and empty.

So: real weather and sea state for a place at a time, from a service, recorded
as observed conditions with their instant and their source. Then "fly it as it
was last Tuesday" is a thing the platform can do, and a sweep can doubt across
a season of real weather rather than three made-up currents.

**Screen:** a place's page shows its water now and over the past month; the
composer offers "as observed" beside "constructed".

**Done:** a dive flown in weather that actually happened, with the record
naming the service and the instant.

## 15 · The surface is a place with weather on it

No waves, no sea state, no free-surface effects — which is why nothing near the
surface behaves properly, why launch and recovery cannot be modelled honestly,
and why the Otter cannot exist. A wave spectrum from the observed conditions of
step 14, and the surface it implies.

This also feeds step 3's caustics, which is why it is here and not earlier.

**Done:** a vehicle at two metres in a two-metre sea does not behave like one
in a tank.

## 16 · Something to say, and a thin pipe to say it through

No acoustic modem, no bandwidth, no latency. This is the constraint that shapes
real AUV autonomy — a few kilobits a second, seconds of latency, and nothing
like it exists here. A controller that would phone home for a decision cannot
be evaluated honestly without it.

**Screen:** on the replay, what was sent and when, and what never arrived.

**Done:** a controller that asks for help gets it late, or not at all, and the
record says which.

# Phase 3 · The vehicle is yours

Two vehicles published, both ours. A vehicle package is 8 KB of JSON with no
mesh in it. The SDK can read vehicles and cannot publish one. A dive pins a
vehicle version and cannot fit it.

## 17 · Sensors that are sensors

The camera renders frames and nothing consumes them. The sonar got ranges
yesterday and **a deployed stack still cannot read them**, because the bridge
publishes the camera, IMU, DVL and depth and stops — so our own controller can
avoid things and a customer's cannot, which is the wrong way round.

  **The sonar reaches the bridge** as a `LaserScan`. The message already
  exists.

  **A CTD**, which needs nothing and has been waiting the whole time, and makes
  the EOS-80 water visible to a controller rather than only to the physics.

  **Multibeam and side-scan**, which are the instruments a survey AUV actually
  carries and the reason anybody flies one.

**Done:** somebody else's controller avoids a mooring block using the sonar.

## 18 · A vehicle you fit

A dive already carries `fitted` — "what of the vehicle's own suite is fitted for
this dive" — read by the runtime and with no screen anywhere. Finish it:

  **Every sensor draws power**, so shutting one down buys endurance and the
  battery arithmetic says how much. Today `hotel_w` is one flat number and the
  BlueROV2's package does not even declare a battery.

  **And so does the computer.** There is no onboard computer modelled at all:
  no Jetson, no power budget, no thermal limit. A controller needing an A100 to
  hit 10 Hz runs fine here and is impossible on the vehicle. A package declares
  what it carries — `{kind, tops, watts, ramGb}` — that wattage joins the hotel
  load, and a stack declaring what it needs *onboard* is refused at admission
  the way a missing sensor already is.

  **A fit is a thing you compose and pin**, like a mission: the same hull
  without its DVL is a different problem and should not need a second vehicle
  in the catalogue.

  **And therefore a doubt.** *What if we fly without the Doppler log* becomes a
  sweep dimension like any other.

**Screen:** a vehicle fit editor beside the mission builder — sensors on and
off with their draw, the computer, the battery, and what the endurance becomes.

**Done:** two dives of one mission, one with the DVL and one without, composed
in the application, and the endurance differs.

## 19 · Bring your own vehicle

The protocol exists and is exercised by the end-to-end check — found, upload by
digest, confirm, version with a manifest, dynamics, publish — and **none of it
is in the SDK**, so today a customer emails you a JSON file.

  `coral-city publish-vehicle ./my-auv/`, wrapping those calls.

  **The hull mesh comes from the package**, so a customer's vehicle looks like
  their vehicle. Today the physics vehicle and the visual vehicle are two
  different things and only one is versioned.

  **Validation, because nothing checks anything**: an inertia tensor that is
  not positive-definite, thrusters that do not span the commanded axes, a
  centre of buoyancy below the centre of gravity. A bad package is currently a
  dive that behaves absurdly with no explanation.

**Screen:** the fleet page shows what a package declares and what was refused.

**Done:** somebody outside this repository publishes a vehicle and flies it.

## 20 · The SDK can only command a wrench

The runtime's `Command` has the third form — `actuators: dict` — and the SDK's
does not, so a person outside can fly a BlueROV2 and cannot fly a Seaglider:
the vehicle this whole phase was built around is closed to exactly the people
whose controllers are supposed to be the point. In the tank, the note somebody
left themselves and never finished:

    command = Command(wrench=asked) if asked.shape[0] == 6 \
        and len(self.model.thrusters) != 6 else Command(wrench=asked)

Both branches are the same expression, in the first file an outside developer
reads.

**Done:** somebody outside writes a glider controller, tries it in the tank,
and deploys it.

## 21 · The rest of the fleet

REMUS 100 and the BlueROV2 Heavy are written and unpublished — packaging, not
physics. **The REMUS is the survey workhorse of the industry**: eight hours of
lawnmower at a metre and a half a second, and you cannot fly it. The Otter
follows once step 15 gives it a sea to pitch in.

**Done:** four vehicles published, and a mission flown by each kind that suits
it.

# Phase 4 · It can be trusted

## 22 · The physics is held against something real

The formulation is the standard one — Fossen's 6-DOF, properly implemented,
with its two approximations stated in the code rather than buried (diagonal
added mass, and Coriolis dropped, which is defensible for a slow ROV and not
for a REMUS at 1.5 m/s). Two hundred and forty-eight tests, many named after
physical facts.

**And not one coefficient has ever been checked against a real vehicle.**

That is exactly where the reef was in September: correct in form, unanchored to
a measurement. The reef was fixed by going and finding the survey. This needs
the same — a published trial dataset or a tank test for one vehicle, and the
model held against it.

**Screen:** a vehicle's page says what its numbers were validated against, or
says nothing was.

**Done:** one vehicle's drag and added mass reproduce a published trial to a
stated tolerance, and the others say plainly that they have not been checked.

## 23 · Something comes out of it

A deliverable is a score and a video. Nobody can take *where the corals went*
out of this and use it, though the outplanting knows to the centimetre.

The georeference exists: a place carries `extent` in EPSG:4326 and a vertical
datum, and the site frame is a square of `acrossMetres` centred on the origin,
so local metres to lat/lon is exactly determined.

  Outplant positions and identities as **GeoJSON**.
  A colony inventory with what was seen and when, as **CSV**.
  A cell's coverage as an **ESRI ASCII grid** with its `.prj`.
  Written in the dive, as artefacts, beside the recording.

**Screen:** a run page that offers them, and says what each is.

**Done:** somebody without this platform opens what a dive produced, in a tool
they already use, and does something with it.

## 24 · Missions that are plans of work

Three of the four missions are not. One has no layout at all and carries a
parametric cell; one has a layout attached that its stage does not use; one is
a single stage I wrote as a test fixture. Only the September plot round — survey
a named cell, inspect a named frame, return — is the real article.

Written with a reef programme, at both sites, for both vehicle kinds, using the
launch and recovery of step 12 and the deliverables of step 23.

**Done:** four missions somebody who dives would recognise as their week.

# Phase 5 · It can be judged

## 25 · The benchmark

Four controllers exist — the planner, the planner with its eyes open, the one
that thinks, the one that asks a model — and **they have never met**. Step 19
of the first plan produced the first comparison anybody would argue about:
`wary` hits nothing and arrives five metres short; `pursue` arrives exactly and
ploughs through three nursery frames. Which is better is a real question and
the platform cannot answer it.

A task set, flown by each, in the same water, from the same seeds, with the
same fit — and energy, time, closing navigation error, things struck, and for
the ones that think, seconds and tokens.

## 26 · The dashboard

The one screen the first plan never touched. Autonomy is still a deploy log
answering "what have I uploaded", which nobody asks.

It becomes the benchmark's screen: each controller with what it has flown, what
it scored, what it cost, over which tasks and in which water — and two of them
side by side, pose by pose, on one dive.

**Done:** somebody picks a controller for their mission because of what this
screen says.

# How this is kept honest

**No step is done without its screen.** Stated at the top and repeated here
because it is the rule most likely to be quietly dropped, and because the first
plan dropped it and left a terminal that can do a dozen things the application
cannot.

**Nothing judged from memory.** Step 1 exists only to make the rest of Phase 1
arguable. Every claim about an image is checked against the same four frames.

**Water before assets — as a prerequisite, not as a priority.** ~~Buying
photogrammetry colonies and looking at them through the fog in today's frames
is paying to photograph fog.~~ Written before step 2 was built and checked, and
wrong: the water turned out to be a hue shift over an untextured ground. The
medium still has to be stated before anything seen through it can be judged,
which is why step 2 stays where it is. But the order after it should follow the
frames, and the frames say **ground and colonies**.

**Looe Key is the calibration; the others are the test.** It is the only place
with ground truth at the scale of a camera frame, so it is where "is this
right" can be answered rather than argued. The other four are where it is found
out whether the machinery generalises to a place with no orthomosaic — which is
every place a customer will bring.

**Thirty metres, not three kilometres.** That is all a camera ever sees
underwater. The sphere around the vehicle is the only part anybody judges.

**Validation is a phase, not a footnote.** Phase 4 is where this stops being a
demonstration. A platform whose physics has never been held against a real
vehicle is making claims it cannot support, and it will be asked.

**What would show this plan is wrong:** a reef scientist picking the render out
of the step 7 comparison immediately; the step 2 water looking right at Looe
Key and wrong at Al Fahal, which would mean the parameterisation is fitted
rather than physical; the step 22 validation failing badly enough that the
vehicle model needs rebuilding rather than tuning; or all of it landing and the
first person who dives saying the missions are still not what their week looks
like.
