# The eighth plan: the places module

*Proposed 6 October 2026.* A twin is only as real as its seabed. Today a place is made by about fifteen command-line tools run by hand in the right order: `get-bathymetry`, `get-reef`, `coral-atlas`, `icesat`, `fit-depths`, `fit-colour-depths`, `make-site`, `reef-survey`, `benthos`, `ground`, `surroundings`, `fauna`, `aim-water`, `check-depths`, `check-places`, with `built.py` and `rebuild` remembering how. They work, and six places came out of them, but each knows only its own source, and nothing combines two sources for the same square metre.

r8 turns them into one module: **places**. It takes whatever data a site has and builds the place from it. AI comes in where it earns its place, behind the same interfaces as everything else.

## What it is

```
sources  →  layers  →  fusion  →  place  →  scene
```

- **Sources** are adapters, one per kind of data. Each fetches or reads its data and hands back layers: Seabed 2030 (GEBCO and its type grid), the DCDB multibeam mosaic, ICESat-2 ATL24 depths, Sentinel-2 imagery, the Allen Coral Atlas, USGS and NOAA DEMs, iNaturalist and OBIS records, and a client's own GeoTIFF, XYZ points, multibeam grid or photogrammetry orthomosaic.
- **Layers** are one shape for everything: a grid on the site's frame with, cell by cell, a value, an error, and the source it came from. Depth, coral cover, habitat class, colony positions, colour.
- **Fusion** decides, cell by cell, which source to believe: the one with the smallest error where they overlap, blended at the seams, and recorded per cell. Models live here too: today's colour-to-depth fit, tomorrow a learned one, each a model that turns some layers into another layer with an error that was checked on held-out data.
- **The place** is the fused layers plus what grows on them: colonies from a survey where there is one, from zonation where there is not, and the fish recorded there.
- **The scene** is what a dive opens: `seabed.f32`, `seabed.usda`, `coral.usda`, the textures, and `site.json`, with every number's provenance.

A place is described by a **recipe**: the site (centre, size, cell), the sources to use, and the models to run. `places build <recipe>` makes it; the recipe is kept with the place, so rebuilding is running it again.

## Build order

1. **The structure.** The `places` package: `Layer`, the source interface, fusion, the recipe, and the scene writer taken out of `make-site`. The existing tools become sources and models inside it, one at a time, each checked by rebuilding a place it built before and comparing.
2. **Sources we already have,** moved in: GEBCO and DCDB (from `surroundings`), ICESat-2 (`icesat`), Sentinel-2 (`get-reef`), the Allen Coral Atlas (`coral-atlas`), USGS surveys (`reef-survey`, `benthos`), fish records (`fauna`).
3. **Fusion with provenance per cell,** and a map of where the place is weak: which cells are measured, which derived, with what error.
4. **Client data in:** GeoTIFF, XYZ, multibeam grids, an orthomosaic.
5. **Models:** today's depth fits as the first; then a learned depth model trained across sites against ICESat-2 and multibeam; then coral cover from imagery with a confidence.
6. **A Place Builder tab:** point at data, watch the place build, see where it is weak.

- *7 October 2026, later.* The terracing fixed: get-reef reads Sentinel-2 bilinearly and is pinned to the scene a place was built from, the median colour is read bilinearly, and the atlas classes are softened where the colour fit draws (not where it fits: fitted softened, a class with no photons took a coefficient of hundreds of metres). Shushah (v3) and Al Fahal (v9) republished: steps over half a metre between neighbours halved and a third fewer, held-out errors unchanged; Shushah's coastline redrawn from a smooth near infrared moved its dive start 150 m.

- *7 October 2026, evening.* Step 5 began as its own project, `ml/depth` (configs, dataset build, U-Net, evaluation, a container on the box's second GPU). depth-v1: 3,267 chips of Sentinel-2 over NOAA reef lidar in six regions, 35 M measured cells. unet-v1, tested on regions it never saw: 1.33 m in Florida (the per-pixel linear fit, 5.58 m), 1.58 m in the Virgin Islands, 3.56 m in Guam and the Marianas, 2.1 to 2.4 m on the Red Sea's ICESat-2 tracks. - *8 October 2026.* The Red Sea in training: three ICESat-2 areas trained on, weighted ten to one against lidar cells, NEOM and Thuwal held out. unet-v2-redsea-w10 beats the per-pixel fit there (1.94 m against 2.30 m). Al Fahal: 0.79 m held out against 2.01 m, better at the photons in every depth band; republished as version 10. Shushah: 1.11 m against 1.28 m held out but worse at the photons in every band; kept as published. unet-v2-final (all five Red Sea areas) is for places outside them.

- *8 to 10 October 2026.* Step 5's coral half, as the two use cases asked for on 8 October (not merged, each its own purpose):
  - **Coral on every map, from the Allen Coral Atlas.** Density per Atlas class, the mix per Atlas zone, one table for every map (`tools/zonation.py`). Colonies come from a coral library built once per region (`tools/coral-library`, `tools/coral_hd.py`): Red Sea (Porites, brain, Pocillopora, Acropora, tables, plates, Galaxea, Millepora, leather coral, Favites, Lobophyllia, Stylophora, Seriatopora, Fungia, Dendronephthya, Xenia, Sinularia, sponges, fans, rubble), Hawaii (Porites compressa and lobata, Montipora capitata, Pocillopora meandrina) and the Caribbean (Orbicella, grooved brain, staghorn, elkhorn, Agaricia, sea rods, Gorgonia fans). Each colony carries colour patches and darkened crevices, and the reef's dead framework is laid on the hard ground. Found and fixed: colonies had been set at the corner height of their grid cell, a metre under the drawn seabed on average, so most of the reef was buried in every dive; and dives now begin where colonies stand. Published: Al Fahal v32, Shushah v13, Kaneohe v24, Looe Key v19.
  - **A dive video into 3D, in its map.** COLMAP reconstructs a stretch of video (`ml/reconstruct`); `tools/place-reconstruction` puts it in a place: at known coordinates, else where its coral cover best matches the map's, else the middle; laid on the drawn seabed's slope, its rim draped, the colonies under it removed, a fixed camera on it. Two patches of the DeepReefMap Red Sea video are in Al Fahal (60 to 180 s and 180 to 300 s; 300 to 420 s failed to reconstruct, 17 of 156 frames). Open: the scale is assumed (camera 1.5 m over the reef), and the fixed cameras view a patch poorly.

- *10 October 2026.* The reconstruction's scale measured, and the patches made to look like the reef they sit in. The scale comes from the video's own motion sensor (GoPro accelerometer and gyroscope, aligned with the camera path; gravity comes out at 9.78 m/s², the check): 1.72 m per unit for the first patch, 2.87 for the second, against 1.35 assumed. A dive can now begin anywhere (`start` in `dives_start`), so one begins over the patch. In it the patch read as a dark blue speckled sheet: its colours still carried the video's water, which the renderer then adds again, and its surface is crumpled (a tenth of its faces point down). Now it keeps the photograph's brightness, takes the reef's tint, and is lit off a smoothed copy of itself; it reads as ground in the reef, though flat at seven metres. Published as Al Fahal v35.

## The camera (closed)

Asked for on 10 October, outside this plan, and closed here so it does not run on. The picture a dive takes now goes through the camera that took it (`services/sim-runtime/coral/optics.py`, `metering.py`), set per vehicle (`camera` in its `dynamics.json`) or per dive (`camera` in the objective): the vehicle's own camera or one the dive carries, motion blur from its movement, a filter, the lens and port (a GoPro fisheye behind a flat port, or the BlueROV2's 80 degree lens behind its dome), vignetting, sensor noise at the actual ISO, white balance (auto, graded towards the reference footage, or locked), sharpening, and video compression at the camera's bitrate. Also: the GoPro look as the default (sunlight keeping half the water's cast, sand greyed), and the water's haze going out at night with the daylight. The runtime image is rebuilt from a clean export and the old one kept as `r1-balanced-2026-10-06`.

One thing left, and it needs the user: a calibration of the real camera (a few frames of a checkerboard), to replace the published field of view and the idealised fisheye.

## What is left of r8

2. **Step 6, the Place Builder,** as a screen and as MCP tools, agreed on 10 October:
   - **The screen.** Places → "New place" → a world map, where you click a reef and size the square → the data for that square, found automatically and ticked (GEBCO, Sentinel-2, ICESat-2, the Allen Coral Atlas) plus "Add your own" (a depth map, soundings, a photo mosaic, a dive video) → Build, watching the steps → the result: measured cells green, guessed ones red, then Publish.
   - **The MCP tools.** The same steps without the screen: build, watch, see where it is weak.
   - **Places belong to an organisation,** private to it unless shared, and what it uploads counts against its limit.
   - **The order:** (0) storage; (1) ownership; (2) a build runs on the box as a job and says each step; (3) the MCP tools; (4) the screen.

   *Step 0 done, 10 October.* Storage stays MinIO on the box (it speaks S3, so moving to hosted S3 when the first outside customer uploads changes settings, not code). An organisation now holds what it uploads, against a limit (200 GB unless the platform sets one), and can let go of it; an object nobody holds and nothing is made of is collected, record and bytes, and one a published place uses is kept. Uploads of more than 32 MB go through the API in parts, so they work from outside the platform's network and resume after a drop; one file may be 64 GB. Tested over the public link: 70 MB in three parts with one held back and sent later, counted, released and freed. Open: the client cannot hash a 4 GB file in a browser at once; it needs a streaming hash when the screen is built. Run recordings and job outputs are the platform's and count against nobody.

   *Step 1 done, 10 October.* A place can belong to an organisation (`POST /organisations/{org}/cities`, `ownedByOrg` on the place). Its members curate it: they record and publish its versions and share it, without the platform. To anyone else it does not exist: not listed, absent when asked for, its files refused, a dive in it refused. A package's files and its publishing are now decided on the place they belong to, and defining a dive now checks that the caller may see its place, which it never did. The platform's catalogue is unchanged. Checked live against the box with two institutions, 19 checks (`tools/e2e`, "A place an institution founds is its own").

   *Steps 2 to 4 done, 10 to 11 October.* A place is built from its data as a platform job (`POST /cities/{id}/builds`). The control plane runs the places module's own image (`packages/places/Dockerfile`, `iocean_places.job`) with a brief: the square, the public sources, the organisation's uploaded files. The image may fetch public data because it is the platform's. Each stage is printed as a line of JSON, which the worker passes on while the job runs, and the package becomes a draft version with where it is weak kept on the build. MCP has `places_sources`, `places_build`, `places_build_status` and `places_publish`. The client has Places → New place: a satellite world map, the data to tick, your own files uploaded in resumable parts, the steps as they come, and the result with Publish. Checked live: a 1 km square near Thuwal built from Sentinel-2, the coral atlas and GEBCO in about two minutes, published, and a dive flown in it (score 1). Open:
   - ~~ICESat-2 needs the NASA token in the worker's settings~~ (done 11 October: the token goes to the worker, which hands it to place builds).
   - ~~A built place is bare seabed~~ (done 11 October). After the seabed the job runs `tools/make-site` and `tools/aim-water`: ground from the coral atlas, corals from the library, water.
