# iOcean, worlds first: the direction (2026-11-03)

Agreed in conversation on 2026-11-03 while reviewing 0.3.1 running locally.
Supersedes the place designer and the "arrangements" of
`app-shape-2026-11.md`. Built in 0.4.0 (see "Where it stands"); 0.3.1 was never released.

## The idea

Opening iOcean is like opening a game: you choose a world and you are in it.
A world is one place in the sea, built in 3D from real foundations that live
in the platform, in layers you can see and edit. Inside the world you design
it, put things in it, write its missions, dive it and replay the dives.
Outside the world are the workshops that make the things a world is built
from, and the library that holds them.

Reference points: Maya, 3ds Max and Unity for the editor; Petrel and GOCAD
for a subsurface model in which surveys, wells and plans are all objects of
one model; Photoshop for layers.

## Decisions

1. **A world is a scene of layers, each a real asset.** Not a baked package
   with its sources thrown away. Layers:
   - water: depth, current, visibility, temperature
   - terrain: an imported bathymetry, or one built from public sources
   - seabed: sand, rock, seagrass
   - reef: coral from the atlas, or a reef reconstructed from video
   - structures: pipelines, frames, transponders, moorings, ships
   - life: fish
   - missions
   The package a dive runs in becomes an export of the scene, not the
   source of truth.
2. **One world, one design.** The place-to-many-arrangements relation is
   dropped. A world has a history of versions to go back to, not parallel
   variants. Want another variant: make another world.
3. **Nothing enters a world unless it is in the library.** Dragging a GLB or
   a GeoTIFF onto a world runs it through the importer first; it becomes a
   library asset, then it is placed.
4. **Missions belong to their world.** A mission is an asset of the world it
   was built in, tied to that world's assets (inspect *this* frame, follow
   *this* pipe). There is no copying a mission between worlds for now. A
   mission pins the world version it was saved against; when the world has
   changed since, it says so.
5. **Edit locally, render remotely.** The editor is a 3D viewport in the app
   (three.js on WebGPU): orbit, select, move, rotate, snap to the seabed,
   layers on and off, instanced corals with level of detail. Instant, works
   offline, costs the shared GPU box nothing. A "look through the camera"
   action asks the simulator for a real render of that viewpoint. Physics
   checks while placing (fit, drape, drop) stay in the client; dives run the
   coupled engine.
6. **Workshops stay outside the world.** Anything that is a heavy job with
   its own steps and yields a reusable thing:
   - Vehicles: hull, thrusters, sensors, figures
   - Controllers: code, builds, versions, deploy, import from GitHub
   - Video to 3D: hours of GPU, yields a reef or a structure
   - Importers: bathymetry, models, routes
   Each yields one finished thing in the library, made without knowing
   which world it will go into. A vehicle in a dive is a pinned version of
   its workshop record.
7. **A dive is one form.** No "mission" versus "single task". In order:
   1. World (already chosen when started from inside the world)
   2. Mission: this world's missions, or "none, fly it by hand", or "new
      mission", which opens the editor
   3. Vehicle, checked against what the mission needs (sonar to follow a
      pipe, and so on)
   4. Flown by: a controller or by hand
   5. Water: defaults to the world's water layer; changing it is a scenario
   6. Positioning: offered from what the world has (laid transponders give
      LBL, a ship overhead gives USBL, else dead reckoning)

8. **Where on Earth is optional.** Every world has its own frame: metres
   east and north of an origin, and everything in it is placed and worked
   out in metres. That is enough for a virtual world (a tank, a designed
   flat, a test site). A world may also have a georeference: its origin's
   latitude and longitude, and true north.
   - It is needed for anything tied to the real Earth: importing a
     georeferenced layer (a GeoTIFF bathymetry, the coral atlas, GEBCO,
     Sentinel, ICESat, a video with real navigation), real water (a buoy,
     an ocean model), deliverables in WGS84, the sun where and when it is.
   - The first georeferenced layer sets it, or you set it by hand before.
   - A virtual world can be given one at any time.
   - Once a world holds georeferenced layers it cannot be moved: the data
     belongs to that spot, and moving it would be a lie. The same design
     somewhere else is a new world.
   - Things with no place of their own (a structure, a model, a reef from a
     video without navigation) go into any world, virtual or real.

## The app

| Entry | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Worlds** | First screen: choose a world or make one. Inside: the editor, missions, dives, replay |
| **Library** | Your assets, with the Store as a section of it |
| **Vehicles** | Workshop |
| **Controllers** | Workshop |
| **Reconstructions** | Workshop: video to 3D |
| **Dives** | Every dive across worlds, to compare (a controller developer runs one controller in many worlds) |

Later, Vehicles and Controllers may fold further.

## Review notes to carry into the build (2026-11-03)

- Home is not a table. A dashboard: what is diving now, machines free, this
  week's results, what is building; your worlds as pictures; a feed of what
  happened (dive started, runs finished, world saved, object ready,
  something failed). No "open to read" anywhere.
- "Runs" is called **Dives** everywhere; New run is New dive. One count:
  the side bar and the page must agree (they said 331 and 257).
- Names agree with the page: Missions has New mission, Search missions, a
  Mission column. No New plan.
- The bar starts on the same line as the content under it; the title is not
  jammed into the corner.
- No buttons in table rows (no Fly, no Open). Click the row.
- A place picker lists your worlds only, never every place the platform can
  see (it showed 14 where you have 6).
- Mission stages explain themselves: a small drawing and one line of what
  the vehicle does; choosing one says what to draw ("click a frame", "draw
  a line"). Inspect takes a real thing in the world, not a free circle. No
  paragraph under "checked against": duration and battery. Reorder stages
  by dragging; duplicate and remove in a "…" menu.
- Charts and pictures: one blue ramp with hillshade and thin depth
  contours, like a nautical chart. Not beige blobs (the Jeddah airport tank
  looked wrong).
- Cards: every one has a picture, one clean line, depth and state. No
  internal ids ("built from the place city_01M2E…"), no essays.
- New dive page must not lose its controls when scrolled.

## Order of work

1. **The world document.** Its format: layers, each pointing at a library
   asset with a transform; missions as entries. Agreed with the auv session
   before anything is built, because the simulator must read it.
2. **Audit the six places.** For each: which foundations still exist in the
   platform, which were consumed or deleted, what must be fetched again.
   Then decompose each into real layer assets.
3. **A read-only 3D viewer.** Open a world, see its layers, turn them on and
   off.
4. **Editing and import.** Place, move, rotate, snap; import bathymetry
   (GeoTIFF, XYZ), models (GLB, OBJ, USD), Store items.
5. **Missions in the world.** Stages drawn in 3D over the terrain and the
   structures.
6. **The app around it.** Worlds as the first screen, Library, workshops,
   Dives, the one dive form, Home dashboard.

## Where it stands (2026-11-03, 0.4.0)

1. The document: world/1 (`world-document.md`), validated by the control
   plane; a place has one world, saved as versions (migration 0045). A dive
   pins a world version, flies its bake and reads its structures as things.
2. Audit: `world-audit.md`. Al Fahal's reference folder is written as
   importable files by `tools/world-foundations`; cover maps and species
   lists are asset kinds (migration 0046, import image r11).
3. and 4. The world editor (`screens/World.tsx`, `parts/World3D.tsx`): the
   terrain in 3D with a chart drape, layers with eyes, structures chosen,
   taken from the library, put down, dragged and turned (heading), removed;
   the terrain stack, water and seabed in the inspector. Objects with models
   are laid into the package on Save, and the bake is recorded.
5. Missions belong to their world and open in the mission designer, pinned
   to the world version. **Not done:** stages drawn in the 3D view; the
   designer is still the 2D chart.
6. Home, Worlds, Dives, Library; the one dive form (world, mission or none,
   vehicle), with Rehearse for sweeps.

Through MCP: `worlds_get` (a place's world, written from the place until
one is saved) and `worlds_save`. `tools/make-worlds` imports Al Fahal's
foundations and the tank's species, then saves the six places' first worlds.
